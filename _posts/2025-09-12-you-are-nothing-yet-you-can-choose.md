  <h1>You Are Nothing, Yet You Can Choose: A View to Live By</h1>
    <p>Here is a way to look at life — three key points that can reshape how you see yourself, your roles, and your emotions. This perspective challenges conventional thinking about identity and offers a liberating framework for authentic living.</p>

  <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1532693322450-2cb5c511067d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80" alt="夜晚月亮的寧靜力量">
  <p class="image-caption">Night moon - the quiet power of choice</p>

  <h2>1. You Are Nothing</h2>
  <p>Everything you think you are, is just an idea.</p>
  <p>You are not a "friend," but you have an idea of being a friend, and if you are authentic enough, you play that idea out. Being born in a middle-class family, being the older brother — all of these are ideas. They are the ways we define ourselves, but none of them capture who we truly are.</p>
  <p>So how do you distinguish between ideas that liberate you and those that trap you? You observe and learn, with curiosity. You may think pursuing a noble goal is always good — but even that can be twisted. You may think listening to your heart is the answer — but sometimes that view is too narrow. The key is accepting feedback, not only from others but also from yourself. And then, learning through art, literature, and life itself.</p>
  <p>Now, does saying "you are nothing" risk leading to nihilism? Nihilism asks, "Why do I even live?" But since life itself has no fixed meaning at the human level, meaning is in your hands to create. The trap is in always searching for things to do, just to prove your life has meaning. Life should be lived, not merely calculated. You can plan, yes, but you must also embrace. Simply being here is enough.</p>

  <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518709268805-4e9042af2176?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80" alt="身份的流動性">
  <p class="image-caption">No restriction</p>

  <h2>2. You Are Always Playing a Character</h2>
  <p>When your boss tells you that you've done poorly at your job, it is not you who failed. It is the employee-character that made a mistake. Maybe you just didn't play that role well in that moment. Right or wrong are always subjective. The guidance is simple: be authentic in how you want to play the role, and keep learning and observing so your idea of that role improves.</p>
  
  <h3>How do you stay authentic while switching roles?</h3>
  <p>Have a clear idea in your mind, so you can present it outward. Misunderstandings will happen. A good purpose may come out as a poor action. That is why we try and fail — it's natural.</p>
  
  <h3>What if your authenticity clashes with social expectations?</h3>
  <p>For example, as an employee with a boss? This is part of discovering who you are and who you want to be. There's no single right answer, only answers that fit you. If going to work requires you to become someone totally different, and it makes you suffer, that is a complicated problem. There are many ways to solve it, but one thing remains true: acting out your idea is the key to improvement.</p>
  
  <h3>Is there a core actor behind the roles?</h3>
  <p>There are personalities — patterns you use by default when adopting characters. They tend to stay with you long-term, influencing how you act across all roles.</p>

  <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1507003211169-0a1dd7228f2d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80" alt="角色扮演的藝術">
  <p class="image-caption">Be authentic</p>

  <h2>3. Emotions Are Weather</h2>
  <p>Emotions come and go like weather. Anger, sadness, joy, fear — they pass through. Depression is more like being stuck in a role you no longer want to play, or being trapped in too much negativity attached to an idea. Still, remember: it is not your fault. You don't have to hold on to emotions forever.</p>
  
  <h3>What if someone feels the need to suppress emotions?</h3>
  <p>We all feel emotions every minute, often shifting from one to another. One practice is to note your emotions 3–5 times a day, and write down what you are doing at that moment. You may realize your total happiness is greater than you thought, and that your down times are just small portions of your day.</p>
  
  <h3>Are positive emotions like joy or love also "not you"?</h3>
  <p>Yes. All emotions — joy, sadness, anger, love — are ways of releasing energy, just in different forms. There are no bad emotions, only actions that can go wrong when taken under their influence.</p>

  <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1506905925346-21bda4d32df4?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80" alt="情緒如天氣變化">

  <h2>The Power of Choice</h2>
  <p>If you accept these three points — that you are nothing, that you are always playing a character, and that emotions are like weather — then you discover something important: <strong>you always have the power to choose</strong>.</p>
  <p>You no longer need to rebel or sabotage yourself just to prove you have choice. The choice was always there.</p>
